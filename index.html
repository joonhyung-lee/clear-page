CLEAR: Capability-Aware Latent Ordering for Adaptive Interaction with Movable Objects

Anonymous Authors / Under Review

TL;DR

CLEAR jointly selects objects, determines their interaction order, and generates motion references conditioned on robot morphology and scene geometry.

Same scene, different bodies

Robot morphology determines terrain traversability and feasible object interactions.Planning must account for these constraints and for how each interaction affects subsequent actions.

From the body to a plan.

Given a scene and robot embodiment , CLEAR proposes plans . The scene includes the observed geometry, movable objects, the robot state and a task goal. Each plan entry specifies an object and its target configuration or trajectory. Among candidates accepted by the task's feasibility checks, the planner selects one with the fewest object interactions.

(b) Latent Interaction Ordering

From , OrderNet predicts a selection probability and priority mean and standard deviation for each object . A sampled indicator determines participation. Sorting sampled scores from low to high gives the order .

Sampling admits alternative object sets and orders for the same query. Selection considers the complete sequence, including objects that become reachable after earlier interactions.

(c) Rank-Causal Generation

With fixed, CausalFlowNet integrates from Gaussian noise in the active coordinates of to . In , row stores coordinates for object . Fixed and padding coordinates remain unchanged. The selected rows are decoded in order into references . Flow time describes generation progress, not execution time.

All selected rows evolve over the same flow interval. Rank causal attention lets each row read its own and preceding ranks. Context tokens cannot read generated rows, preventing later interactions from influencing earlier ones through .

(d) Execution and Replanning

Decoded references are checked in order , accounting for the scene changes caused by earlier interactions. The planner selects a candidate that satisfies the goal with the fewest interactions among those accepted. A valid plan requiring no object interaction bypasses generation. MPC tracks the object references using updated state estimates. When enabled, replanning uses the updated scene to revise the remaining interactions.

CLEAR evaluates candidate commands through policy rollouts, retains high performing and behaviorally diverse candidates, and combines Cartesian tracking with joint command smoothing. The controller executes an initial segment and updates its plan using new observations.

Scene and protocol

A UR5e arm manipulates a cube, two buttons, a drawer, and a sliding window. Button requirements determine when the drawer and window become available. The evaluation tests unseen combinations of those requirements. All methods share an MPC controller and replan after interactions.

This scene compares CLEAR with classical search, behavior cloning, a shared grounding variant, and separately trained ablations of affordance conditioning and causal attention. Goal completion and precondition violations are reported separately for each displayed episode.

The interactive replays preserve recorded joint configurations and button states. Results shown under individual clips are not aggregate success rates.

Scene and protocol

The evaluation considers G1, Spot, Spot with an arm, and Husky in shared environments with terrain and movable obstacles. Standard scenes vary traversability and interaction requirements. High mass scenes include objects weighing 11.5 kg. Each embodiment uses a fixed controller across planning methods.

The available replays below isolate high mass interactions for G1 and Spot with an arm, followed by a terrain comparison for G1. The full evaluation additionally measures planning accuracy for Husky. These selected scenes illustrate individual behaviors rather than every evaluated trial.

Execution replays use the recorded physical states. When a planner returned no valid plan and no trajectory was recorded, its tile shows the initial scene with a start marker. It does not simulate an unobserved failure.

Validation and replanning depend on the experimental protocol. Grid and Ordered Manipulation omit separate feasibility validation. Maze Navigation comparisons omit high-level replanning.

Scene and protocol

The experiment compares CLEAR with a variant that retains its initial plan. Both share the learned checkpoint, controller, and execution limits. The paper evaluates nominal execution, displaced target objects, and changes in access to later interactions. Low level feedback alone cannot revise the high level interaction sequence.

The paired scenes below are qualitative physical replays with external object relocations. Their archived plans were transferred and validated for these scenes. They illustrate recovery behavior and are not the trials used to calculate the paper’s aggregate results.

Each replay retains its own recorded simulation duration. The pair is not time synchronized. Object relocations are external disturbances, not robot interactions.
